Privacy.

A plain-language explanation of what this small artist website processes and why.

01approach
small site.
minimal data.
02contact

What you send.

If you use the collaboration/contact form, you provide your name, email address, topic and message. That information is used to read and respond to your inquiry. v3breaks.com does not intentionally create a separate public-facing contact database from these submissions.

03security

Cloudflare + Turnstile.

The site is delivered and protected through Cloudflare. The contact form uses Cloudflare Turnstile to distinguish people from automated abuse. Turnstile may process technical signals such as IP address, TLS fingerprint, user-agent information, the site key and the page origin for bot detection and security.

Cloudflare Turnstile privacy information ↗
04email

Message delivery.

Contact-form messages are sent through Resend to the private v3 breaks inbox. To deliver those messages, Resend processes information contained in the email, which can include your email address, name, message content and delivery metadata.

Resend privacy policy ↗
05analytics

Traffic + analytics.

This site does not use advertising pixels or sell contact-form information for targeted advertising. Cloudflare may provide technical and aggregate traffic/security analytics as part of hosting and protecting the site.

06links

Music platforms.

Links to Spotify, Apple Music, Bandcamp, YouTube, SoundCloud, TikTok, Ko-fi and other external services take you away from v3breaks.com. Those services have their own privacy practices and terms.

07retention

How long.

Contact messages may remain in the project inbox and the services used to deliver them for as long as reasonably needed to respond, keep necessary correspondence, maintain security or handle project administration. Messages can be deleted when they are no longer needed.

08requests

Questions or requests.

If you want to ask about information you previously submitted through the site, use the contact form and choose “Other”. Put “privacy request” in the message so it is easy to identify.
